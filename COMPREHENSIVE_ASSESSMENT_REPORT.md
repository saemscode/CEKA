## 2. Detailed Meeting Proceedings & Outcomes

The meeting was the first Year 3 activity under UNFPA's MASS programme, funded by Global Affairs Canada (CAD$5M/USD$3.6M). It operated as a participatory workshop, heavily featuring open-floor discussions, Q&A, and survivor-support panels.

### 2.1 Confirmed Outcomes and Forward Commitments
*Note: These are stated priorities by COVAW/MASS staff during the session.*

1. **Harmonized Reporting Protocol:** A commitment to consolidate multiple disparate reporting processes into a single, unified protocol to reduce friction for survivors navigating different services and platforms.
2. **COVAW/MASS Year-3 Provisions:**
    *   **Community of Practice:** Establishing a coordinated space across three counties for joint action and data sharing on TFGBV.
    *   **Data-Collection / Operational Network:** Mapping the legal landscape ("what laws do we have, where are we heading") and defining organizational roles within that network.
    *   **Capacity-Building Strategy:** Expanding training sessions for service providers beyond the previous one-day format.
3. **Training Expansion:** Acknowledging that the previous one-day training (which reached ~270 participants) was insufficient, future modules will be expanded to at least four days. Module 2 is planned across Nairobi, Mombasa, and Kisumu.
4. **Internal Policy Review:** COVAW challenged attending organizations to audit their own internal HR and conduct policies regarding online harassment, pushing for internal accountability alongside external advocacy.
5. **Digital Rights and International Law:** A significant discussion occurred regarding whether digital rights are covered by the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) or African conventions. It was noted in the room that Kenya has *not yet ratified* the AU Convention on Ending Violence Against Women and Girls (2025), which explicitly includes cyberspace violence.

### 2.2 Key Themes from the Transcript
The high-fidelity transcript captured several recurring themes relevant to CEKA's work:
*   **System Complexity:** Survivors struggle with the "double complexity of the system" and the platform of reporting. There is a strong need for simplified, accessible information (a core CEKA competency).
*   **Community Guidelines vs. Reality:** Debates on what constitutes a "community" online and whether users actually read or understand platform community guidelines.
*   **Legal Anchoring:** The necessity for policy-makers and support services to have legal anchors for their activities, highlighting the gap between existing laws (like the Sexual Offences Act of 2006) and modern digital realities.
*   **Financial and Resource Constraints:** Mentions of the financial costs associated with reporting and seeking justice, and the need for funding to support grassroots campaigns.
