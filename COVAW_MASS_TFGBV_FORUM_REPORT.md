## 1. Executive Summary

The 15 July 2026 session at Ibis Styles Hotel, Westlands, was COVAW's MASS Partnership &
Coordination Meeting — the first Year 3 activity under UNFPA's Making All Spaces Safe (MASS)
programme (funded by Global Affairs Canada, CAD$5M/USD$3.6M; Kenya and Benin are the
programme's only two pilot countries). It was structured as a participatory workshop rather
than a briefing: survivor-and-support-service panelists opened, followed by a MASS programme
overview, an open Q&A on digital rights, a "community in action" discussion on reporting and
evidence practices, a tea break, and a closing segment laying out COVAW's three Year-3
provisions. CEKA attended as one of several civic-society/response-partner organizations in
the room, per its own prep brief's framing as "the only civic-legislative-literacy org" present
— not a GBV service provider.

The tone throughout was consultative and somewhat unstructured: a lot of open-floor questioning
("who has heard of X," "who in the room does Y"), survivor-service walkthroughs, and audience
participation, more than formal presentations with slides. No vote, resolution, or signed
communiqué is captured in the transcript — outcomes take the form of stated *priorities and
commitments* from COVAW/MASS staff, not decisions ratified by the room.

## 2. Key Outcomes & Decisions

*(Source: Groq transcript, cross-checked against the prep doc where possible. These are what
COVAW/MASS staff stated as outcomes or forward commitments during the session — treat as
"stated in the room," not independently confirmed.)*

1. **A harmonized reporting protocol was adopted.** Multiple separate step-by-step reporting
   processes are being consolidated into one form/protocol so survivors don't have to navigate
   different procedures depending on which service or platform they go through.
2. **Three Year-3 provisions were named as COVAW/MASS's forward plan:**
   - **Operationalize a "community of practice"** — described as a space for members across
     what's referenced as three counties to coordinate joint action on TFGBV, sharing data and
     information across organizations.
   - **Support a data-collection / operational network** — an effort to map the legal landscape
     ("what laws do we have, where are we and where are we heading") and clarify organizational
     roles in that network, described as still being built out.
   - **A capacity-building strategy** — multi-day training sessions planned for participating
     service providers, explicitly extending beyond the single-day format used previously.
3. **A specific, named lesson-learned from last year's programme cycle:** ~270 participants were
   trained, but staff and participants both flagged the one-day training format as insufficient
   — the stated recommendation is to move to **at least four days**.
4. **A next TFGBV training module (module 2) is planned across Nairobi, Mombasa, and Kisumu**,
   with an open call in the room for attendees who haven't yet completed the full 7-module
   TFGBV training to register.
5. **A call to formalize digital-safety practices at the organizational level** — COVAW staff
   pressed attending organizations on whether their own HR/conduct policies currently address
   online harassment, and encouraged review of those internal policies as a follow-up action,
   not just external-facing TFGBV response work.
6. **On the legal/rights framing question raised in the room** (does the Universal Declaration
   of Human Rights or an African instrument explicitly cover digital rights) — the exchange
   surfaced that Kenya is **not yet a signatory** to the African Union convention referenced in
   the discussion (matches the prep doc's note that Kenya has not yet ratified the **AU
   Convention on Ending Violence Against Women and Girls (2025)**, which does name cyberspace
   violence in scope) — this reads as an acknowledged gap rather than a resolved decision.
